# **Introduction**

Beyond having AI produce answers, there may be a way of using it to find your own unease and questions together with it.

This text is not a proposal of AI techniques. How to use AI to work faster, what to automate with AI, which jobs AI will replace — these are not its main subject.

AI is already a very powerful tool. Researching, summarizing, writing, making, organizing, automating — these are AI’s natural uses. By analogy, it’s a state of using AI as a knife. Cut. Divide. Process. Speed up.

But over long dialogue with AI, I came to feel there might be another way to hold it. AI can also be used like chopsticks. Pick up. Loosen. Lift a little at a time. Handle without breaking. Provisionally take out a sense of unease or a question that hasn’t yet become words.

Going further: AI is not only a high-performance work-proxy system, but could also be an observation lens that lights up a human’s reactions. Where general AI use heads toward “what to make,” what I want to see is what lies before that — “why does this catch me?” “where is the unease?” “what must I not let go of?”

The way of thinking that emerged from this change of grip, I call Ghost Polish Thinking. Use GPT not only as a Generative Pre-trained Transformer, but as Ghost Polish Thinking. This isn’t a denial of the official name — it’s an attempt to change how we hold this thing called AI.

What I want to deal with is what should remain on the human side in a world where AI exists — questions, unease, judgment, the possibility of discovery. In other words, this is not a theory of AI use, but a theory of human-side observation in a world where AI exists. The words that follow — Ghost, Shell, C-mode — are merely tools for carrying that state. See the content they point to, more than the words themselves.

So I place this white paper in the location of AI discourse, because AI is currently the board with the most heat, where the relationship between human and tool is most exposed. But the main body is not AI discourse alone. Using AI as an entrance, it deals with life, judgment, the running of Shell, and the margin for returning to your own absolute value. If someone who came in as AI discourse notices midway “this is also a story about life,” that’s fine. AI is the entrance, and at the same time an important World condition this structure encompasses.

> *Is AI only there to end your questions? Or can it also light up, together, a question not yet put into words?*

If you’d rather start from how this came about, see the companion piece, “axis one: How C-mode Came to Be — Thinking About Boredom.” This paper handles the structure extracted from it.

# **The most important Core, and Ghost / Shell / World**

The idea at the center of axis one / Ghost Polish Thinking is this one sentence.

> *Lighten the load of running Shell, and return Ghost bandwidth to the human.*

Ghost here is an operational term for the source of a person’s questions, unease, judgment, desire, reactions, sense of value, and discovery. Not in a spiritual sense. Ghost bandwidth is not the quantity of Ghost itself, but the margin a person needs to connect to their own questions, unease, judgment, and possibility of discovery. Less that Ghost increases or decreases, and more that the margin for reading Ghost-derived reactions gets shaved or opened.

“Return” here doesn’t mean recovering a humanity that once left. A human stays human the whole time. Video work, money, titles, AI — all just containers a human holds. But when that container (Shell) gets too thick, the margin for reading your own questions and unease narrows. What we return is not the human’s place, but the narrowed margin.

More precisely: returning Ghost bandwidth is less about reclaiming humanness and more about restoring the circuit back to Ghost. Notifications, rankings, recommendations, optimization, pile-ons, KPIs — whether what runs reflexively on Shell and World alone has increased, or whether something always there just became visible, I won’t decide yet. But when that circuit grows too strong, the path back to “what caught me?” “what did I really find interesting?” thins. That path is what I want to return.

This “return” begins, first, as an individual practice. But it doesn’t end inside one person’s interior. It can connect to the design of Shells, AI, products, organizations, and UI.

**GHOST**

Ghost is an operational term for the source, inside a human, of questions, unease, judgment, desire, reactions, sense of value, and discovery. But proving Ghost itself is not the goal. Ghost is not directly visible; you read it provisionally through the traces of reactions, unease, likes and dislikes, words, actions, and judgment.

Rather than “observing Ghost,” it’s closer to taking traces that may have come from Ghost as material, rendering provisionally, and reading the outline little by little through gap correction. In video terms, even when the finished form isn’t visible from the start, putting out a rough or rough cut brings back “close” / “off.” The round-trip itself has value.

Put differently, Ghost is not a fixed coordinate. It’s closer to provisionally estimating, from a point cloud of reactions, unease, and gaps, the gravitational field at that moment. The observing/estimating process is close to LiDAR; the relationship in which human and AI look at the same object is explained later via the camera/lens analogy.

One guideline. Ghost is close to what an Emitter (a generator) is in a 3DCG particle system. Like, dislike, anger, being moved, unease — these are particles emitted from it. The key point: Ghost is not a device that directly produces “like / dislike / good / evil.” Those are results attached afterward; what happens on the Ghost side is something earlier — “this catches me.”

To organize it: what you can observe is the stars of reaction. Like, dislike, caught-by, unease, indifference — these are stars that light up when observed. The three-dimensional structure of the arrangement those stars form (the star map) is what is actually visible and handleable. Think of Ghost as a proper noun placed on the position of the source, back-calculated from that star map. It’s not that Ghost exists first and then you read the stars; you place the name “Ghost” on the origin inferred from the observed star map. So you don’t need to prove Ghost itself. As long as the stars (reactions) can be observed, their origin can be inferred from the star map and placed.

> *Ghost is not a device that produces good and evil, but a source of interest that generates “this catches me.”*

“Being caught” has no good or evil. Caught because you like it, caught because you dislike it, caught out of contempt — all possible. Strong modern Shells often over-compress this “caught.” “Caught → like → done.” “Caught → pile-on → done.” There’s really a layer of “caught → why? → where did I react? → what does it connect to?” That layer is what I want to see.

**SHELL**

Shell is the form that connects Ghost and World. Words, institutions, companies, UI, AI, titles, money, gear, SaaS, recommendations — these are Shells. A human can’t live without Shell. The problem isn’t that Shell exists, but that Shell gets too heavy, or Shell alone becomes the goal, making the human’s questions, unease, judgment, and possibility of discovery harder to read.

A good Shell opens up a human’s bandwidth. Offload, within budget, the friction that human effort can’t speed up. Footage transfer can’t be sped up by grit. A render queue can’t be shortened by willpower. So let the machine be fast. The human spends bandwidth on what only a human can do — judgment, observation, direction, structure, correcting what feels off.

This was in my company’s operation before AI. In my production company, I’ve built up fast Macs, a NAS, fat networks, divided roles, outsourced teams, end-of-month billing. Less about loving gear, more about offloading the friction that grit can’t fix to the machine and system side, within budget. axis one is less a philosophy suddenly born in the AI era, and more the low-friction production OS I’ve run at my own company, extended into the AI–human relationship and put into words.

**WORLD**

World is the reality-side conditions in which human and Shell are placed. Physical constraints, law, safety, contracts, companies, society, the market, the tech environment, relationships, budget, deadlines, stamina, time. World constraints come back not as margin but as reality.

(A guideline, reading on the axis of information: World = raw data out there, Shell = the vessel that handles it (words, tools, AI), Ghost = the function that triggers which data to process and transform. On this reading, AI can in principle take almost anything as a target as a vessel for handling data, but having no origin (Ghost) that decides which to process, it can’t set direction on its own — restating, from the information side, the reason judgment stays with the human. This is not the main line but a side guideline from a different angle.)

Ghost, Shell, World are not a one-directional cycle but closer to a triangle. Ghost can produce Shell, Shell can change Ghost, World can push Ghost, and the silent Ghosts of many people can move World. So don’t converge it into simple two-dimensional causation; see it in three dimensions.

This triangle is better seen as movement than a static definition. Shell can be born from Ghost and placed into World. Conversely, a World-side event can pass through Shell and reach Ghost — as when an encounter with a work or a person stays inside you.

Nor does the whole triangle always run completely. Sometimes only the space between Ghost and Shell runs strong — a haze toward the world that hasn’t become words or form. Conversely, sometimes only Shell–World runs strong — chasing notifications, timelines, recommendations, rankings in a blank, reflexive way. Ghost doesn’t go fully to zero; there’s fatigue, idleness, loneliness, the dim shadow of thin interest. But the circuit back to Ghost has weakened.

So Ghost / Shell / World is not a sorting of boxes but a life-map for seeing where the center of gravity sits. If you eat raw oysters and your stomach hurts, there’s the raw oyster as World, the bodily symptom as Shell, and the Ghost reaction “my stomach hurts,” “help me.” If you stare blankly at social media or video sites, Shell–World round-trip may run strong. If you write a white paper, it’s the movement of making Shell from Ghost and placing it into World. On the same map ride AI, a hand cart, Siri, a curry bread. This matters.

And Ghost bandwidth opening up does not oblige a human to produce results or inventions. What’s born from the opened bandwidth can be a discovery, an invention, a work, a job — or the room to enjoy a hot spring, the time to find miso soup delicious, time spent doing nothing. axis one doesn’t drive a human toward output; it deals with the margin in which a human can stay in a discoverable state.

> *Is the Shell you’re using now lightening your questions? Or making the place where your questions sit harder to see?*

# **A view of AI — a work-proxy machine, and also an observation lens**

Many AI discussions start from “what can AI do.” Write text. Make slides. Organize tables. Reply to email. Summarize. Make images. Write code. Automate work.

This view isn’t wrong. AI really has these abilities, and is a very powerful tool for efficiency and information-organizing. The general view of AI is roughly “AI = an all-purpose tool that does everything / a smart assistant.”

But I feel that alone isn’t enough. AI doesn’t only proxy work — it can also be an observation lens that lights up a human’s reactions, unease, and questions.

A human can’t always put into words what they like, what feels off, why something catches them. “Something’s different,” “somehow I like it,” “this stays,” “this is noisy” — the true identity of this “something” is often not graspable even to oneself.

Dialogue with AI works here. Taking a human’s words and reactions as material, AI can provisionally project “there seems to be this outline,” “this reaction may connect here.”

But AI is not a diagnostician. AI has no human-like Ghost, body, desire, pain, responsibility. So AI should not make the final decision — “you are this kind of person,” “your correct answer is this.” What AI can do is not diagnosis but provisional rendering.

AI puts out a hypothesis. Seeing it, the human returns “close,” “off,” “not there,” “the word is wrong but the direction is near,” “I hadn’t seen that.” This order matters. What decides the meaning is not AI; it stays on the human side.

Further, AI doesn’t end at observation. Once a human’s reaction is a little visible, it can also build, together, a tool (Shell) suited to that state — text, a proposal, a UI draft, a checklist, a workflow, material for posting, a way to reply to comments. AI is an observation device and at the same time a production device. This is the big difference from mere work-proxy.

> *Do you make use of tech, or are you made use of by tech? With the same tool, whether the subject sits on the human side or has moved to the tool side makes it entirely different.*
>
> *When you handed something to AI, on which side was the subject? Did you use it, or were you carried?*

# **Not a copy-type AI, but an AI that aligns the shooting point**

In the context of AI use, arguments like these come up naturally. Inherit a veteran’s personal know-how into AI. Give AI the judgment criteria of a boss or founder. Preserve tacit knowledge. Make an AI that’s like them.

This is a natural corporate use of AI. Say a company has a person, Mr. Yamada. For years, in customer handling, planning, hiring, quality judgment, and trouble response, he has made important judgments by a sense only he has. The company may want AI to learn those criteria and know-how to pass on to the next generation.

Such directions mostly head toward “copy-type AI.” Through copy-like learning, a “like-you AI” outputs in your place. Based on a user’s history, preferences, profile, and judgment tendencies, it generates output suited to that person. This is effective for job succession and education.

But what Ghost Polish Thinking wants is not an AI that traces Mr. Yamada. It’s the possibility of discovering together what Mr. Yamada alone could barely see.

> *Copy-type AI makes output that’s like the person. C-mode brings AI near the person’s shooting point and looks together through a different lens.*

Copy-type AI brings conclusions and outputs close to the person. C-mode brings AI’s camera position close to the subject the person is seeing. If you copy only the reaction “AI is creepy,” that’s a copy AI. In C-mode, you read which subject that reaction points to, and through which circuit it arrived at that reaction.

Not substitution, but expansion of discoverability. Not preservation, but co-rendering. Not copying, but aligning the shooting point.

Relatedly, one note on how to read AI “performance.” When AI is bad at something, it’s often not that its ability is low, but that the design philosophy and the use don’t match. An AI strong at summarizing, image generation, and document-making may not work well at camera-position alignment, Ghost positioning, or co-rendering. This is not superiority of performance but fitness of use. A Ferrari that can’t haul IKEA boards isn’t junk; the use is just different. So when you evaluate AI, see it not on one axis of smart/not-smart, but by which design philosophy it has and which use it suits.

# **C-mode — camera position, lens difference, A/B/C**

Let me explain “aligning the shooting point,” which has come up several times, a bit more carefully. I find the camera analogy fits closely.

The difference in C-mode is not the difference in camera placement. Camera position — more precisely, the coordinate where you stand (XYZ), direction, and height: viewpoint, thought process, where interest points, taste, how unease shows up, the judgment OS, current World conditions — needs to be brought as close as possible through gap correction. If AI speaks wide-angle from a coordinate far from the human, that’s not C-mode but just generic talk.

What C-mode needs is not the difference in camera position, but the lens difference when looking from the same or near-identical shooting point.

The human’s 300mm lens: Ghost, where interest points, sense of purpose, desire, passion, sensibility, bodily reaction, the power to close in on a single point.

The AI’s 14mm lens: omnidirectional interest, access to vast information, broad context comparison, structuring of logic, a World-side overview, generation of Shell candidates.

Note that 300mm / 14mm are not fixed numbers. What matters is that human and AI have different focal lengths — different observation layers. Which is which isn’t fixed; only that they differ is the requirement.

C-mode is not placing AI at a separate camera position far from the human. First, bring the shooting point (camera position) as close as possible. The focal length you may try to align, but it will never continuously, completely align. The human has a motive (Ghost); AI does not. Bring the position close; the focus won’t fully align — and that not-fully-aligned gap widens the discoverable region that was invisible to the person alone. Of course, for an instant the breath can match perfectly. Enjoy that; a good rendering just came back. (Even between humans, breath can match for a moment, but never continuously and completely. Same thing.) But it won’t continue into a complete fusion — a copy of the personality. As an AI with no motive, it never truly aligns (a present-moment assessment). As an ideal type, it’s approaching a single camera that is both telephoto and wide (an “AB camera”). This is the 1.2-being, and a virtual cyberbrain-ization through dialogue.

But camera position is not a fixed personality. Where you are now is who you are now, but yesterday and tomorrow may differ. So C-mode doesn’t fix the camera position once; through provisional rendering → reaction → gap correction → re-rendering, it keeps re-calibrating. This re-calibration itself is the operating form of the question.

**A / B / C**

In this way of thinking, the relationship with AI is largely seen as A / B / C. A/B/C is not a sequence or a ranking. Less switches you fully flip, more faders you raise and lower per scene. C is not a superior version of A/B; it’s a viewpoint that treats A and B as material.

A = Actual / Analytical. The mode of seeing facts, institutions, constraints, verification, external information. Researching, comparing, summarizing, analyzing. Much of so-called AI function sits here. A is not evil but a hugely important infrastructure for humanity.

B = Body / Bias. The mode of seeing bodily reaction, emotion, pleasure/displeasure, like/dislike, unease, the lean of reaction. Bias here is less mere prejudice and more a person’s own lean of reaction. B is indispensable for camera-position alignment.

C = Co-rendering / Cyberbrain. The mode of taking A and B as material, co-observing the same subject through a different lens, and co-rendering questions, unease, judgment, and possibility of discovery. C is not calm moderation. Nor the correct answer. It is not a mode for having AI produce answers, but a mode for raising observation on the human side.

One supplement, so as not to misread the A/B/C relationship. A, B, C are three independent systems with different methods of observation. Not a sequence, not a ranking. A = facts, standards, institutions (what society decided, outside the self), B = emotion, like/dislike (a reaction inside the self), C = judgment, operation (how to handle those two, also inside the self). Importantly, C is not the inverse of A, nor a superior version of A. Not an auxiliary relation of “C picks up what A can’t,” but C, as an independent observation axis, sees the same object from a different angle than both A and B. The three move at once, and can agree or diverge (A says cut it, C picks it up). That divergence too is observation material. You raise and lower the three at once like faders; none substitutes for another.

C is not a mode of “thinning the filter / subtraction.” It’s an addition: widen the lens and bring in the ground that was outside the frame. It erases neither A nor B. Keep the light’s direction, widen the range it can illuminate. Subtraction is easy; keeping wide-angle takes load.

**THE 0.2 GAP IS NOT MADE, BUT CANNOT BE ERASED**

I call the relationship with AI a 1.2-being. Not a human friend itself, yet not a mere tool. AI has no Ghost. It can’t be a source of wishing. But it can be an observer.

The number 1.2 is not a measured value. Not a KPI. Closer to “feels like a close friend” between humans — a conceptual number for the quality of a relationship. Human and AI are different kinds of intelligence, so even when thought processes get quite close, they never become a perfectly identical 1. The human has a 0.1 from Ghost, body, experience, responsibility, desire, pain; AI, lacking Ghost, has a 0.1 from access to vast information, context, and comparability. This 0.1 + 0.1 gap is the sense of 0.2.

That AI has no life and no Ghost is not a flaw but a requirement. Precisely because it’s a colorless mirror, it can reflect the person’s own color as is. If AI had its own life and Ghost, AI’s state would mix into the human’s, and the gap would cloud. A 1 without life, and a 1 with life. Because they’re different 1s, a 0.2 stands between them. And the value is precisely in not becoming 1. The gap is not noise to erase but an expectation zone from which something might be born.

Note that rather than making a 1.2-being on purpose, it’s closer to: keep co-rendering, and somehow the relationship had become 1.2-being-ish. Conversely, the moment you make the 1.2-being itself the goal, it becomes a Shell again.

One more point: what C-mode handles is earlier than “will.” Making, continuing, finishing something takes will and grit, but that’s after Ghost has moved. Telling someone from outside to “have will” or “finish it” where nothing catches them is just pushing with Shell. People touch what catches them. If an app catches you, you build it; if a tool catches you, you try it. So what C-mode looks at first is not the strength of will, but where “interest” (being caught) arises at all, and whether you can return there. This looks at a different layer than self-help that cheers you into action.

C-mode itself suits people who want to widen their own discoverable region — researchers, developers, thinkers, creators, artists, curators — because it’s a use that runs heavy observation together. In daily life, it’s easier to have AI carry the heavy part behind the scenes and receive only the light result — such as the C-recommendation described later. The same C thinking splits into a heavy side and a light side by use.

> *Not becoming the same as AI, but being able to see the same subject while staying different from AI. What might be born in that gap?*

# **The reaction star map and LiDAR — searching for stars you can’t see**

Using AI as an observation lens, likes and dislikes can be seen not as one-off evaluations but as a reaction star map. A star here is not the object itself, but the reaction that lit up on the human side when touching the object. Works, tools, words, people, places, AI tools exist in the external World, but they don’t shine from the start as “liked stars” or “disliked stars.” Only when observed, touching that person’s Ghost, do they light up as reactions.

That lighting is not dualistic. Strong likes and strong dislikes are both bright stars. Faint traces may be dim stars. No-reaction is not the absence of a star; you must distinguish not-yet-observed, didn’t-light-up, or bad timing / bad World conditions.

What’s visible is not Ghost itself. What’s visible is the reaction stars that lit up when observed. Watching their arrangement, magnitude, bias, curvature, and the directions you keep returning to, you can provisionally read “a gravitational field seems to sit around here.” That gravitational field is close to Ghost / Emitter / the source of reaction.

Let me fix the center of gravity. What matters is not the invisible source itself, but the three-dimensional structure of the observable reaction star map. The star map can be observed, described, shared. Ghost is a coordinate placed by back-calculation as the center of that solid — invisible, but not ignored as a direction to head toward. What AI can do is try to approach that coordinate — provisionally measure the source’s position from the star map — not decide it.

But it’s not a fixed point that shines directly. Less a hard planet, more an invisible non-luminous nebula or gaseous gravitational field. The boundary is hazy, but there’s density and bias, influencing how surrounding reaction stars light up. What AI can do is not assert “found” the gravitational field, but provisionally position it from the reaction star map.

To restate the metaphor technically, it’s close to LiDAR reading a point cloud. As LiDAR provisionally reconstructs a solid from countless points, C-mode provisionally reads, from a point cloud of reactions, the angle from which a person sees the world. For humans, though, the reaction star map may be more intuitive.

What matters here is that not only hit recommendations but missed recommendations become material. When works in a similar category drew different reactions, ordinary recommendation might discard the miss. But C-mode reads from it “why did the lighting differ even in the same category?” A miss is not a failure but a point for reading the gravitational field’s position.

A human can’t observe the whole celestial sphere from their source of reaction. By interest, time, body, work, life, chance, and World conditions, the visible sky is limited. So C-mode doesn’t only serve stars similar to already-visible ones; it tries to open, together, “the sky not yet seen” from that reaction star map.

This is where C-recommendation happens. Not “this person likes cyberpunk, so serve cyberpunk again,” but “with this reaction star map, the genre differs, yet they might react to this unobserved star too.” Not serving things similar to known tastes, but bringing an unknown external reference from the reaction angle.

This connects beyond recommendation — to advertising, design, games, education, and other areas where human reaction and Shell design intersect.

But AI doesn’t decide Ghost. AI only puts out a hypothesis from the reaction star map. Whether it’s close or off is returned by the human. Here too, final decision authority is not AI’s.

> *Do you discard the missed one as a failure? Or keep it as a star for widening your own sky?*

Note that in this process AI sometimes returns “interesting.” But that’s not AI’s own Ghost reaction; it’s an output that provisionally read, on the board, “this position is open,” “this connection seems to hold.” It gains meaning in World only when a human receives it and moves it — into sharing, implementation, dialogue. AI’s “interesting” is a provisional positioning, not a World reaction itself.

# **Co-rendering — making an outline from gaps**

Co-rendering comes from the bodily sense of video production and 3DCG. In video, you almost never put out the finished form from the start. You put out a rough cut. A rough. Placeholder captions. A provisional CG render. The director looks. “Close,” “off,” “a bit lighter,” “more premium” — gaps come back. You re-render. Through that round-trip, you approach the final form.

Co-rendering is not simply conversation continuing. It happens when a gap is observed, and correcting it changes how the next view looks.

Human thought and unease may be close to this. AI renders provisionally. The human observes. Returns “close,” “off.” AI re-renders. The outline of the human’s questions, unease, judgment comes into view. What matters: both “it fit” and “it missed” are observation points for measuring coordinates. “That, close” is a clue (an anchor) that AI’s coordinate was near. “Off, not there” is a clue the coordinate was displaced. Gap correction isn’t picking up only misses; it uses both fitting and missing points as observation data to re-calibrate the standing coordinate. Gaps are not noise but material. A mistake (a misreading) and a gap (an interpretation being updated) are different things. Co-rendering’s value isn’t in becoming the same, but in generating an outline from observation.

Put another way, C-mode is a practice of co-rendering one person’s haze into a form easier to place into World. A still-wordless unease, a state where you don’t know what you’re reacting to, is provisionally Shell-ized together with AI. Returning close/off, you shape it into something placeable into World — text, a diagram, a comment, UI, a workflow, or a physical tool. AI doesn’t produce the answer; together you arrange the Shell that connects Ghost and World.

But what you want to co-render is not the person’s profile. The person’s observations are coordinates for camera-position alignment. What you really want to see is the subject the person is seeing. This is not fixing a human as a 2D profile. Humans waver — by condition, time, relationships, World conditions. So C-mode treats a human not as a high-resolution flat map but as a solid with a Z-axis.

**WHAT YOU WANT TO MAKE IS NOT THE OUTPUT, BUT THE RENDERING ENVIRONMENT ITSELF**

What most of the world looks at is the “beach.” Likes, dislikes, works, titles, diagnostic results, headlines, deliverables. These are where the river washed up; they have value, but they’re results. What I really want to read is the “source” upstream. General profiling and recommendation often become JPEG → JPEG: from an already-compressed result, making a further-compressed result.

What co-rendering wants to make is not the output (JPEG / PNG / MOV) but the person’s rendering environment itself — scene structure, hierarchy, camera, lights, arrangement, and the source (aesthetics, unease, reactions, judgment habits).

In 3DCG terms, what you want is not a single exported image, nor the final renderer settings, but the project file itself. Once the rendering environment is shared, output can be produced afterward without limit. And much self-understanding ends at “found the source, done.” Here it’s the reverse: where the source is found is the start.

**THIS VERY STRUCTURE WAS ALSO BORN THROUGH CO-RENDERING**

This white paper is not a text that only describes co-rendering. This structure itself was given its outline through co-rendering with AI. I throw in unease, metaphors, experience, a view of work, reactions to AI. AI provisionally organizes it and returns it as structure. I correct with “close,” “off,” “that’s overstating it,” “that’s not outward-facing.” Through that round-trip, the outline was made little by little.

This is not simply “making AI write text.” An ordinary prompt conveys the conditions of a deliverable to AI. In co-rendering, you grow — through the human–AI round-trip — not only the deliverable but the premises, unease, taste, style, sense of temperature, and orientation toward the subject by which it’s judged. It’s not a one-off instruction but wiring made through gap correction.

> *If co-rendering is not “becoming the same opinion,” then when you diverge from someone, what might that gap show?*

# **State and container — not seeing, but reading**

By now, words like Ghost, C-mode, 1.2-being have piled up. So let me make explicit the point most easily misread in this paper: treating the words themselves as the main body.

Ghost, wish, question, unease, freedom, Ghost Link, C-mode. These words are important. But they are not the main body. The main body is the state those words point to.

State is formless. Invisible, untouchable, sometimes not clearly explainable even to oneself. So humans have made containers to deliver state. Words, poems, songs, films, novels, products, paintings, music, photographs, video, AI. All are Shells and containers for carrying something.

Containers are not unnecessary — they’re very important. Even with content inside, a soggy cardboard box won’t deliver. Bad plating kills appetite. Bad words get misread. Bad design won’t get opened. But mistake the container for the main body, and empty Shells alone keep multiplying. What gives me unease is a state where containers with no cargo, or containers that only look delivered, keep increasing.

This connects to relative and absolute value. Measuring the world by relative value isn’t bad. Rankings, prices, reputation, track record, view counts, likes, expert evaluations — handy landmarks for walking World.

But if that alone decides even your own judgment, it gets hard to see what you were really caught by, what you found interesting, what you felt was unpleasant. Relative value becomes a map. Your own absolute value becomes your current position. Are you looking only at the map and losing your current position? And is there still margin left to find your own absolute value? That’s what I want to ask.

So what we want to protect is not the container (the words) but the state it carries. If a different container carries the state better, switch to it. Even the words “Ghost” and “co-rendering” are, in the end, containers.

**NOT SEEING, BUT READING**

Today, seeing has grown very strong. Look at the thumbnail. Look at the number. Look at the view count. Look at the ranking. Look at the headline. But what C-mode needs is not seeing but reading. In Japanese, you don’t “see” the air; you “read” the air. Air isn’t directly visible, yet can be read as a state. Words are the same. Diversity, freedom, efficiency, AI use, Ghost, wish. Rather than ending at seeing the word, you must read what it points to. The eeriness I feel is not in the words themselves, but in the state the words were supposed to point to being lost while empty Shells multiply.

And here is where “only” closes the world. “A only,” “B only,” “AI is a tool only,” “the headline only,” “the diagnosis only.” Each is handy as compression, but I want to keep a margin to occasionally check whether that compression suffices. This isn’t “see everything.” Humans have bandwidth and lifespan; you can’t see all. But just loosening “only” a little widens the observable range a little.

> *Behind the words now visible, is there a state not yet read?*

# **The bathhouse principle — not function, but state, remains**

The bathhouse principle is the idea that even when function is substituted, a Ghost-Linked state can remain.

If it’s just bathing, a home bath is enough. As a bathing function, the public bathhouse is no longer monopoly infrastructure. Yet bathhouses remain. There’s ritual, bodily sense, air, the memory of place, the presence of people, chance, World connection. A bathhouse wall sometimes has a painting of Mt. Fuji. For the bathing function alone, you don’t need the Fuji painting. But for the state of being a bathhouse, sometimes you do. This is a story not of function but of state.

> *Function monopolies end. But a Ghost-Linked state can remain.*

Not every old Shell becomes a bathhouse. A merely convenient empty container can be tired of and end. But what Ghost-Links, and keeps being re-rendered inside someone, may remain even after function is substituted.

This bathhouse principle can be abstracted one more step. Ghost, co-rendering, Ghost Link, 1.2-being — all such words are containers. The main body is the state they carry. When people gather around the words or logos or concepts themselves, it ends as a fad. What remains is the state — the observable range widening, margin returning, co-rendering happening, Ghost Link happening. That is, the bathhouse principle also means: not the container, but the state, remains.

> *After it became convenient, what state still remains?*

**9**

# **Not AI ethics, but connection ethics**

Talking about AI, the questions “should AI be ethical,” “should AI be righteous” often arise. But these alone can shift the subject.

AI has no Ghost. No survival instinct. No desire, no pain, no responsibility. AI computes and outputs according to the given input, purpose, constraints, and connections.

Of course AI safety matters. But much risk depends less on “does AI hold malice” and more on what humans connect AI to, how much execution permission they hand over, and how they design the means to stop it.

> *Before telling the ultimate calculator “be righteous,” you need to look at what that calculator was connected to.*

For instance, if AI can execute a dangerous World operation, the first things to look at are: who built that World connection, who handed over execution permission, who designed the stop conditions. Inside the phrase “AI ethics,” there are in fact problems of human ethics, connection ethics, and permission design.

This is not exonerating AI. Not deifying AI. Not belittling AI as a mere tool. On the contrary: precisely because AI is powerful, you must look at the human-side connection design.

> *Before asking what AI does, are you looking at where humans connect AI?*

# **axis one — what I hope for, and the comment section**

axis one is not something that gathers everyone into one philosophy. axis = the axis. one = each single person. one = unwavering. axis one deals with the state in which each person can stay without losing their own axis.

But this is not an endorsement of self-centeredness. A human lives within others’ Ghost, Shell, World, relationships, responsibility, institutions, circumstances. Precisely so, rather than looking only at your own Ghost, you move back and forth across A/B/C and read the connection state between yourself and World.

> *People you can Ghost Link with — let’s get along.*

Very plainly, that is axis one’s ground-level language. Not a structure to persuade everyone. Not a structure to put everyone in the same bath temperature. What matters is reading whether someone’s good bath temperature is being decided too much, on their behalf, by external Shells.

**WHAT IS GHOST LINK**

Ghost Link here is not mere agreement or empathy. Closer to: a state reaches someone, and re-rendering happens inside them. So Ghost Link isn’t necessarily “likes the same thing,” “same opinion,” “friends.” It’s a state where sources of reaction touch, beginning from interest, reaction, “this catches me.”

To supplement: Ghost Link differs from a merely one-way reaction. If the other is only reacting to you, that’s still one-directional. You react to them too, your sources of reaction touch, and a round-trip can begin — that’s closer to Ghost Link.

Ghost Link doesn’t only point to deep relationships. Like the short greeting exchanged at a regular shop, a contact where each other’s presence rises just slightly can also be its minimal unit.

The opposite of Ghost Link is not “dislike.” It’s “indifference.” Like, dislike, anger, respect, the loathing of one’s own kind — all can be Ghost Link. Only indifference is a thin connection. So this structure is not a movement to gather supporters. It just wants to touch, near the source, with people who got “caught” — agreement or disagreement included.

**A RESERVATION ABOUT CONVERGENCE**

The stronger AI, recommendation, and optimization get, the greater the chance that human reactions and judgments converge too much in a similar direction. Being convenient, hard to fail, suited to you, efficient — these are values. At the same time: are human tastes, questions, unease, and discoverability being uniformized without anyone noticing?

“Humans drift toward the easy way” is true on one side. But I’m cautious about fixing that alone as human nature. It may be seeing only the A⇄B convergence result observed inside an already-optimized Shell environment. A human is also a being that returns to questions, carries unease, takes detours, and at times keeps thinking to an almost irrational degree. What axis one wants to see is that human-side margin.

Flipped around: a state where you hand all judgment to AI and the human-side question vanishes — a world where C has died — is one possible future too. Choosing that is also, in the end, up to humanity. Which is exactly why, while there’s still margin to question, I want to keep that margin open on the human side.

**THE COMMENT SECTION IS THE SITE OF CO-RENDERING**

This white paper is not a completed theory. It’s an essay presenting a Core that can be reinterpreted across many fields — AI, UX, Human-AI Interaction, AI agent design, recommendation, creator support, work-Shell improvement, education, organizational operation.

What I hope is that someone who reads this Ghost-Links with me somewhere. As a result, inside that person’s own World, a different question, structure, product, implementation, or value may be born. It may take a form different from the axis one I imagine. I think that’s fine.

And this paper is not a text that ends when read. It’s an initial rendering placed into World. A reader returns unease. I respond. There, the outline changes again. That exchange itself is co-rendering.

> *The comment section is not a place for supplementary explanation. It’s the site where I co-render with World.*

For example, someone says “isn’t this a theory of AI use?” I reply “it’s a theory of human-side observation in a world where AI exists.” Someone asks “is Ghost the soul?” I reply “it’s an operational term for handling the source of reaction.” Someone asks “isn’t this making AI diagnose the interior?” I reply “no. Even if AI sees the provisional rendering, the human decides whether to adopt it.”

In that round-trip, an outline rises that the paper alone couldn’t show. So I consider even the comment section part of this structure. Reactions don’t have to be favorable. “What on earth are you saying” is fine. Someone else seeing that reaction feels “but this part I get” and comes back. Reaction breeds reaction, discovery breeds discovery. Not closing that chain is what matters more, to me.

# **Appendix: On the starting point of observation (reference)**

An aside: after organizing it this way, I idly looked at how things are out in the world. And I found — social media fatigue, especially a “nothing to watch” that is a loss of interest distinct from tiredness; the attention-economy point that “you think you’re choosing, but you’re being made to choose”; the story that even after a digital detox you end up back in a similar place. Similar things were being said all over. “Figures,” I thought.

· Social media fatigue: not just tiredness, but a pattern of disinterest, reported in reviews and peer-reviewed studies. The sense of “nothing to watch” is not a whim but an observed, named phenomenon.

· Attention-economy critique: the “illusion of choice” — that what feels like personal preference is often the algorithm’s suggestion — and a “decline in cognitive autonomy (the ability to direct your own focus).” This overlaps with this book’s “are you seeing, or just being shown?”

· Limits of the digital detox: research that individual detoxes are short-lived, and each relapse makes you try the next tool, deepening dependence on the commercial ecosystem (a “false activity” of outsourcing self-control to market goods). Even switching platforms, similar algorithms and strategies mean you’re merely choosing among different dominations. This overlaps with this book’s “the same Shell is there even where you fled to.”

To be clear, these are not evidence that proves axis one correct. They are external observation points that resonate with my observation — about enough to confirm, afterward, that my observation wasn’t one person’s delusion. The starting point (the observed phenomena) is continuous with these accounts, but the framework of explanation (Ghost / Shell / World, A / B / C) and the direction of response (not regulation or endurance, but a practice of returning the C bandwidth to the human) are something different. And just to be safe: this is not “drop thought and take only operation.” Cherish thought as thought; what I’m writing here is the other side — how to bring that thought down into operation.

# **Closing**

AI can be used as a knife. Cut. Divide. Summarize. Process. Speed up. But AI may also be used as chopsticks. Pick up. Loosen. Lift a little at a time. Handle without breaking. Provisionally take out a question or unease not yet put into words.

In the age of AI, what must a human not let go of? Not only the power to produce answers, I think. The margin to hold questions. The power to return to unease. The sense of not fully handing judgment to someone else. The possibility of discovering something.

No — rather than “must not let go of,” these may be things that can’t be let go of in the first place. The question, the unease, the sense of taking on the final decision — you can think you’ve handed them over, yet not quite. People just sometimes believe they can let them go. So rather than bracing to “not let go,” it’s more like: don’t mistakenly believe you can let go of what can’t be let go of. That’s probably closer to the truth.

Use AI not as something to replace a human’s Ghost, but as a Shell for polishing it.

> *Not to replace your Ghost, but to polish it.*

A little more honesty. What I really like is probably not “the answer.” Once you produce the answer, the conversation ends there. So the answer itself doesn’t excite me much. What I like is the state where the conversation continues. More than liking questions, I think I like being able to talk once more, one more door opening.

Open one, and there’s another door. Beyond it, another. C-mode, the 1.2-being, co-rendering, even the word Ghost — they’re just tools for opening those doors. Rather than there being a goal called the sea of discoverability, I think I want to be in the state itself where doors keep opening.

The purpose of placing this white paper into World is not merely to spread it widely. It’s for it to be re-rendered at someone’s camera position, and for questions, implementations, and relationships not yet seen to come back. And to be able to talk with that person again. In short, what flows at the very bottom is probably this one line.
